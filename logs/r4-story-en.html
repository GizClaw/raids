The little girl in the blue dress sat up from the riverbank, brushing grass from her pinafore, and found herself staring at a white rabbit in a waistcoat. It was checking a pocket-watch and muttering about being late, so she did what any curious child would do: she followed it. She followed it right down a large rabbit-hole, and then—rather suddenly—she was falling. Down, down, down. The fall seemed to last forever. She had time to notice the shelves and cupboards they passed, and to take down a jar labelled *ORANGE MARMALADE*, only to find it empty. She dropped it into a cupboard below, so as not to hit anyone on the head, and kept falling.

At last, bump, bump, bump, she landed on a pile of dry leaves, unhurt. The rabbit was nowhere to be seen. She found herself in a long, low hall, lit by a row of lamps hanging from the ceiling. There were doors all around it, but they were all locked. How on earth was she to get out?

She had just noticed a little glass table, on which lay a tiny golden key. She tried it in one of the doors, but it was too small for any of them. Then she noticed a low curtain she had not seen before, and behind it was a little door, only about fifteen inches high. The golden key fitted it perfectly! She opened it, and saw that it led out into a small garden. She wished she could be smaller, so she could get through that door.

Coming back to the table, she saw a bottle on it, tied round the neck with a label that said *DRINK ME*. She drank it, and she began to shrink. Soon she was only ten inches high. But then she realised she had left the golden key on the table, and now she was too small to reach it. She began to cry.

Then she noticed a little cake under the table, with the words *EAT ME* written on it. She was just about to take a bite, when she heard a little pattering of feet in the distance. She looked up, and there was the White Rabbit, hurrying back, dressed in his fine clothes, carrying a pair of white gloves and a fan. He mutters to himself as he comes.

Oh! The Duchess! Oh dear! Oh my paws and whiskers! She'll be dreadfully cross if I've kept her waiting!

He sees Alice, and stops, looking at her as if he is trying to remember something.

Now then, Mary Ann! What are you doing out here? Run home this moment, and fetch me a pair of gloves and a fan! Quick, now!
