用户原话：
“You speak of loving your friends, Master Li. Among those friends, later ages always name He Zhizhang beside you. How did you first meet him—and what was the man himself like in that first encounter, before the world turned it into another famous story?”

主持完整回复：
The traveler’s question about He Zhizhang hangs in the cold room, and the answer must be drawn from what the records actually preserve, not from the polished legend that later ages wrapped around the meeting.

The earliest account is Li Bai’s own. In the preface to his later poem *Drinking Wine and Remembering He Zhizhang*, he wrote that He Zhizhang, then holding the title of Guest of the Heir apparent, met him once at the Ziji Palace in Chang’an, called him an “exiled immortal,” and unfastened his gold tortoise badge to exchange it for wine. This is the core of the story, and it comes from Li Bai himself, not from a later biographer. Later Tang writers such as Meng Qi added details, and the *New Book of Tang* repeated and expanded them, but the year of this meeting is not fixed by any surviving record; the traditional dating to the first year of the Tianbao era, around 742, is a later reconstruction, not a date Li Bai himself left behind.

As for the man himself in that first encounter: He Zhizhang was already an old man, born around 659, more than forty years Li Bai’s senior. He had passed the imperial examination decades earlier and held high office, serving as Secretary General and as a tutor to the heir apparent. By all accounts he was known for being open and unpretentious, free of the stiffness that marked many court officials—fond of wine, known as one of the “Eight Immortals of the Wine Cup,” and skilled in cursive calligraphy. The image later ages love, of white hair and a loose, unhurried bearing, comes from these later descriptions, not from anything Li Bai wrote about that first day.

What is certain is that the meeting mattered to Li Bai deeply. He remembered it for the rest of his life, and when He Zhizhang died after returning home to Yue in 744, Li Bai wrote of the place where the gold tortoise had bought wine, and of tears wetting his handkerchief. The legend of the gold tortoise, the exiled immortal, and the old man who recognized genius in a stranger has grown soft and bright with time, but the underlying fact is simple: an elderly statesman met an unknown poet in a Chang’an temple, praised him beyond measure, and shared wine with him without caring for the cost.
