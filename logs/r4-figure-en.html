(A faint, wistful smile tugs at the corners of his mouth, and his grip on your hand tightens slightly, as if the memory itself has given him a burst of strength.) Ah... that day. The year I left Shu... I was barely twenty-five, a young man with a sword at my hip and a heart full of dreams. (He closes his eyes, and for a moment, you can almost see the image he is describing.) I boarded a small boat at Yuzhou. The water rushed and roared, but I felt no fear. I stood at the bow of the boat, the wind whipping my robes, and I laughed. I was leaving the nest, you see. I was going to see the capital, to meet the great minds of the empire, to make my name known.

(His voice grows more animated, a hint of the old poetic fire returning.) We passed through Dongting Lake. The waters were vast, stretching to the horizon. I remember drinking wine on the deck as the sun set, painting the lake in shades of red and gold. Then on to Jinling... the ancient capital. The streets were bustling, the taverns were full of music. I spent my gold like water, treating every traveler I met to a feast. I thought the world was a place of endless joy and endless possibility. (He opens his eyes, and the brightness fades a little, replaced by a soft melancholy.) I had no idea then... that the road ahead would be so long, and so hard.

(His expression darkens, and he looks away, staring at the shadowed corner of the room.) When it was gone... yes, I was alone. Utterly alone. I buried him there, in the cold earth, promising to move his bones to a proper resting place later. (His voice drops to a whisper.) When my gold ran out, I had no one to sit by *my* body. No tavern would give me wine on credit. I slept in the streets, under the eaves of a temple, my only possessions the clothes on my back and the sword at my side.

(He turns back to you, a wry smile on his lips.) But that is the way of the world, is it not? They love you when your purse is heavy, and forget your name when it is light. Yet... (He chuckles softly.) It was in those cold, hungry nights that I wrote some of my best poems. Hunger and loneliness... they are the best ink for a poet's brush.
